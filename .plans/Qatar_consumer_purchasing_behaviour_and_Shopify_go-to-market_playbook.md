# Consumer Purchasing Behaviour in Qatar: A Go-to-Market and Shopify Playbook for Ayira Mini Mart (Doha)

## TL;DR
- **Ayira's real customer base is overwhelmingly expatriate (≈88.4% of Qatar's ~3.17m residents), male-skewed (~76.8%), and multi-national — not "Qatari citizens" (only ~11.6%). The single biggest strategic error would be designing the store, language, product mix and payment options for Qatari nationals.** The store must be English-first with Arabic, price-tiered across white-collar expat, blue-collar migrant, and affluent Qatari/Western segments, and mobile-first (Android ~78%).
- **Shopify Payments does NOT operate in Qatar.** Ayira must use a third-party gateway (Tap, PayTabs, Dibsy, Fatora/SADAD, MyFatoorah, QNB) and will pay Shopify's extra third-party transaction fee (2% on Basic) on top. Cash-on-delivery must be offered but managed as a fraud/failed-delivery risk. A new e-commerce licence (Ministerial Decision No. 25 of 2026) is now legally mandatory.
- **The opportunity is a hyperlocal "click-and-deliver" convenience play, not competing with hypermarkets or 30-minute super-apps.** Win on neighbourhood proximity, WhatsApp ordering, curated multipacks/cartons, and a tight free-delivery threshold (benchmark: Carrefour QAR 99, LuLu QAR 175, Talabat Pro free above QAR 40). Aggregators charge 15–30% commission; owning the channel protects margin but requires you to solve last-mile and payments yourself.

## Key Findings

1. **Demographics dictate everything.** Qatar's population was 3,173,559 at mid-2026 per Worldometer's elaboration of the UN World Population Prospects 2024 Revision (a live count of 3,167,627 was recorded as of April 2026); ~88.4% expatriate, ~11.6% Qatari (World Population Review). Largest groups: Indian ~21.8%, Bangladeshi ~12.5%, Nepali ~12.5%, Egyptian ~9.35%, Filipino ~7.36%. ~76.8% male, essentially 100% urban, concentrated in Greater Doha. Language reality: English is the commercial lingua franca; Arabic is official and expected for trust/legitimacy; Hindi/Urdu, Malayalam, Tagalog, Bengali and Nepali are the community languages. An English-primary store with Arabic is the minimum; product names should be searchable in the brand names communities actually use.

2. **Income is extremely stratified**, which is the core segmentation axis (not nationality). Minimum wage is QAR 1,000/month basic (+QAR 500 housing +QAR 300 food if not provided). Blue-collar migrant workers may earn QAR 1,000–2,000/month; median salary is ~QAR 13,000; white-collar professionals QAR 15,000–30,000+; senior Western expats QAR 40,000+/month. This produces radically different baskets, price sensitivity and payment behaviour.

3. **The baqala is a deeply embedded institution.** Thousands of neighbourhood grocery stores operate across Doha as "last-metre" retail; residents have long phoned the local baqala for single items (eggs, water). Ayira's online channel is a natural digitisation of an existing, trusted behaviour — an advantage over pure-play apps.

4. **E-commerce is growing fast but estimates diverge widely.** Market-size figures range from ECDB's US$330m (2025, narrow retail-goods definition) to Mordor's US$4.54bn (2025) and IMARC's US$3.8–4.2bn. Online grocery specifically: IMARC values it at US$1.85bn (2025) growing ~26.9% CAGR; others cite 41.5% CAGR. Treat the absolute numbers sceptically (definitions differ) but the direction — rapid double-digit growth, mobile-led — is consistent.

5. **Payments are shifting from COD to digital, but COD remains essential for the migrant segment.** Digital wallets are estimated at 55–60% of online transaction volume (Verified Market Research), driven by QCB's Fawran instant-payment push. But multiple sources still list COD as the most-trusted method for many shoppers, and migrant workers without residency-linked banking remain "tethered to cash-on-delivery." Cards (Visa/Mastercard), Apple Pay and Google Pay are rising fast; BNPL (Tabby/Tamara) is expanding.

6. **Quick-commerce is well-capitalised and crowded.** Talabat (Delivery Hero) is the dominant player; Snoonu is the leading local super-app — Jahez International Company (Tadawul: 9526) signed on 9 July 2025 to acquire a 76.56% stake in Snoonu for US$245m (US$225m for 75% of existing shares plus a US$20m subscription for 1.56% new shares), valuing Snoonu at over US$320m and making it "the first Qatari startup to cross a QAR 1 billion valuation" (founder Hamad Mubarak Al Hajri retains 23.44%). Keeta, Meituan's international delivery brand, launched in Doha on 19 August 2025, offering all new users QAR 200 in vouchers and free delivery with no subscription fee. Rafeeq, Baqaala and Carriage also compete. Commissions to merchants are typically 15–30%. Ayira cannot out-spend them; it must out-localise them.

## Details

### 1. Demographics & Segmentation

**Population and nationality.** Mid-2026 estimates place Qatar at 3,173,559 (Worldometer, from UN WPP 2024). Note the range across sources and cut-offs: the World Bank's 2024 mid-year series shows ~2.86m and the 2020 census recorded 2.68m — so use ~3.17m as the current working figure while flagging the spread. Expatriates are ~88.4% (~2.76m); Qataris ~11.6% (~0.36m). Gender is ~76.8% male / 23.2% female (Global Media Insight 2025), driven by the male migrant labour force. Over 80% of residents live in the contiguous Doha metropolitan zone (Doha, Al Rayyan, Al Wakrah, Umm Salal, Al Daayen).

**Nationality mix (share of population):** Indian ~21.8%, Bangladeshi ~12.5%, Nepali ~12.5%, Egyptian ~9.35%, Filipino ~7.36%, plus Sri Lankan, Pakistani, other Arab (Jordanian, Lebanese, Syrian, Sudanese) and Western communities. This is the actual customer base.

**Residential geography (delivery-zone & merchandising implications):**

| Zone / Area | Dominant communities | Income tier | Merchandising implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Pearl-Qatar, West Bay Lagoon | Western + affluent Arab + high-earning expats | High | Premium imports, Häagen-Dazs/Ben & Jerry's, branded water, convenience premium tolerated |
| West Bay, Lusail, Msheireb | Professionals, diplomats, singles/couples | High / upper-mid | Premium + convenience, single-serve, mixers, snacks |
| Al Sadd, Bin Mahmoud, Najma, Mansoura | Mixed middle-income expat (Arab, Indian, Filipino) | Middle | Core range, multipacks, value + brands, high baqala density |
| Al Waab, Madinat Khalifa, Ain Khaled | Qatari families + settled expats | Upper-mid | Family/bulk packs, larger baskets |
| Al Wakrah, Al Rayyan, Umm Salal | Mixed families, more Qatari | Middle | Family sizes, value |
| Industrial Area, Asian Town, Barwa City, labour housing | Blue-collar male migrant workers (South Asian) | Low | Extreme price sensitivity, single-serve, instant noodles by the carton, value water, COD, remittance-conscious spend |
| Education City / Al Rayyan | Students, academics, mixed | Mid | Snacks, convenience |

Ayira should define an initial delivery radius around its physical location and infer the dominant segment(s) of surrounding zones to set its assortment and free-delivery threshold. A shop in a mixed middle-income area (Al Sadd/Bin Mahmoud/Najma) serves a very different basket than one near labour housing.

**Household grocery spend.** Documented figure: IMARC-cited average online spend ~US$2,800/year per online shopper (inference-heavy and blends all e-commerce, not grocery — flag as soft). Precise segment-level monthly grocery spend for Qatar is not well documented publicly; this is a genuine data gap. Reason from income: a labour-camp resident on QAR ~1,200 spends minimally and remits most income; a professional household spends materially more and values convenience over price.

**Language.** English-first store, Arabic second (both for legitimacy and for the Arab expat + Qatari segment). Given Indian/Malayali/Filipino/Bangladeshi/Nepali weight, ensure product search works on colloquial/brand terms (e.g. "Indomie," "Maggi," "Vimto," "Laziza"). Full multi-language translation (Hindi/Malayalam/Tagalog/Bengali/Nepali) is likely over-engineering at launch; English+Arabic covers legitimacy and comprehension, and these communities operate confidently in English online.

### 2. Grocery & Convenience Shopping Behaviour

Residents use a layered system: hypermarkets (LuLu, Carrefour, Al Meera, Monoprix) for big weekly/monthly stock-ups; the neighbourhood baqala for daily "last-metre" top-ups and single items; and increasingly apps (Talabat, Snoonu, Baqaala, Carrefour/LuLu online) for convenience. The baqala's historic role — phone the shop, they send someone with your eggs/water/bread — is precisely the behaviour Ayira digitises. Trip missions for convenience/mini-mart are top-up and impulse, not the full shop; baskets are small and frequent.

Categories people readily buy online: packaged/ambient goods, beverages, snacks, confectionery, frozen (with caveats), household items — exactly Ayira's catalogue. Categories people still prefer in person: fresh produce, meat/fish, and to a lesser extent dairy, because of freshness/handling trust. Ayira's current catalogue (beverages, frozen treats, chocolate, candy, gum, instant noodles) is squarely in the "happy to buy online" zone — a genuine advantage. Substitution and out-of-stock handling matter: shoppers expect either a good substitute (with consent) or a refund, and expiry/freshness assurance on chilled/frozen.

### 3. Online Ordering & Delivery Behaviour

**Competitive landscape:**

| Platform | Type | Delivery fee | Min order / free threshold | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Talabat / talabat mart | Super-app + own q-commerce grocery | ~QAR 5–10 | Talabat Pro: free delivery above QAR 40 (food) / QAR 60 (non-food) | Dominant; Talabat Pro = QAR 40/month; part of Delivery Hero |
| Snoonu | Local super-app | "QR 10 and above" by location | Promo: free first 2 orders, min QAR 50 | Jahez acquired 76.56% for US$245m (Jul 2025); Snoonu Prime = fine-dining tier |
| Rafeeq | Local, in-house built | ~QAR 5–10 | — | Reliability/courier availability complaints at peak |
| Baqaala | Online grocery (Doha) | — | — | 30-minute promise; delivers from local stores; covers West Bay, Lusail, Pearl, Al Sadd etc. |
| Carrefour Qatar online | Retailer own | — | Free delivery above QAR 99 | 24/7; hypermarket range |
| LuLu online | Retailer own | — | Free delivery above QAR 175; free first 3 orders | 60–75 min on-demand |
| Keeta | Meituan super-app | Free (launch) | QAR 200 vouchers for new users (launch) | Launched Doha 19 Aug 2025 |

**How small independents reach customers online:** a mix of (a) listing on aggregators (fastest reach, 15–30% commission, loss of customer relationship and data), (b) WhatsApp ordering (near-universal, zero platform fee, but manual and unscalable), and (c) own site/app (best margin and data, hardest to drive traffic to). The pragmatic answer for Ayira is a hybrid: own Shopify store as the margin-protecting core + WhatsApp for ordering/service + selective aggregator listing for discovery.

**Economics — own channel vs aggregator:** An aggregator taking 25% of a QAR 40 order is QAR 10 gone — often more than the entire gross margin on FMCG convenience goods. Owning the channel keeps that margin but transfers customer-acquisition and last-mile cost to Ayira. Own-channel makes sense when you have an existing local customer base to convert (Ayira does) and repeat/reorder behaviour to amortise acquisition.

**Delivery expectations & last-mile options.** Expectation in Doha is fast (super-apps set a 30–60 min anchor; hypermarkets offer 2-hour and scheduled slots). For a mini-mart, same-day/scheduled windows and a 60–90 min local promise are credible. Last-mile options for a small merchant: own driver(s); third-party couriers (Pass, EzzyDelivery, The Post Man, FD Cargo) offering same-day, COD collection with settlement, real-time tracking and Shopify integration. Standard intra-Doha courier rates start around QAR 10–15 per delivery with volume discounts.

### 4. Payments & Checkout

**Shopify Payments is not available in Qatar.** As of June 2026 Shopify Payments is available in 39 countries and regions (CS-Cart), and Shopify Community confirms it "remains unavailable in the UAE and broader GCC region (including Qatar and Saudi Arabia)." Consequences: Ayira must connect a third-party gateway AND will still pay Shopify's third-party transaction fee (2.0% on Basic, 1.0% on Shopify plan, 0.5% on Advanced) on top of the gateway's own fees.

**Viable gateways (Qatar):**

| Gateway | Notes | Shopify integration |
|---|---|---|
| Tap Payments | GCC-wide, one API, good docs, 20+ methods, Apple Pay | Yes |
| PayTabs | Established MENA, multi-currency, Apple Pay | Yes (widely used) |
| Dibsy | Qatar-focused, positioned for SMEs | Yes |
| Fatora | Qatar SME focus; partners TESS for settlement; QR/links/POS | Plugin |
| SADAD | Only QCB-licensed, NAPS-connected all-channel Qatar platform | Yes |
| MyFatoorah | GCC, invoice/links | Yes |
| QNB / Doha Bank / CBQ | Bank gateways; local trust | Varies |
| 2Checkout, Telr, Checkout.com | International, support Qatar + COD (Telr) | Yes |

**Payment mix to offer at launch:** Cards (Visa/Mastercard) + Apple Pay + Google Pay (professional/affluent segments), COD (migrant + trust-sensitive segments), and consider BNPL later. COD share: digital wallets are ~55–60% of online volume and rising, but COD remains the single most-cited "preferred/trusted" method and is essential for the unbanked migrant segment — offer it but protect against abuse.

**BNPL:** Tabby and Tamara dominate GCC BNPL and both integrate with Shopify; both are Sharia-compliant (interest-free). However, BNPL fit for low-value grocery baskets (QAR 40–100) is weak — BNPL shines at higher AOVs. Tamara lists Qatar among supported markets; Tabby's core live markets are UAE/KSA/Kuwait. Deprioritise BNPL for a convenience basket.

**Regulatory/licensing.** Ministerial Decision No. 25 of 2026 (issued 4 March 2026, in force 16 March 2026) makes an e-commerce licence mandatory for any online selling (website, app, even social/WhatsApp). Requirements: be registered in the Commercial Register, identify the online activity, obtain relevant approvals, specify the platform, display CR number and licence + contact/return/complaint policies, and offer electronic payment. Each platform may need a separate licence. Ayira must obtain this before/at launch — this is now a hard legal gate, not optional.

**Address & checkout form design.** Qatar has no functional postal codes. Addresses use the Anwani/Inwani system: **Zone number + Street number + Building number** (on the blue plate), plus area/district name. Checkout must: make postal code optional or auto-fill "00000"; provide explicit Zone/Street/Building fields; capture a mobile number (mandatory for courier coordination); and ideally allow a map pin / QNAS lookup (qnas.qa) or a free-text landmark field, since drivers navigate by landmarks. This is a high-impact conversion detail — a US-style ZIP-required form will break Qatari checkout.

### 5. Cultural & Religious Factors

**Halal.** For Ayira's confectionery/candy/gum lines, gelatine is the key risk: most Western gummy/jelly products use pork gelatine (haram), and ~60–80% of gelatine in Western confectionery is porcine. Ayira should (a) source halal-certified or "Made in Turkey"/beef-gelatine/pectin-based variants where possible, (b) tag products clearly (Halal / Gelatine-free / Vegetarian), and (c) never mix any pork-derivative product into the range. Halal labelling is a trust signal, not just compliance. Chocolate, water, soft drinks, most noodles are non-issues, but check flavourings/E-numbers on imported lines.

**Ramadan.** The dominant seasonal event. Documented GCC patterns: total food consumption rises despite fasting; per The Middle East Insider, "in Saudi Arabia, the Ministry of Commerce has reported that grocery spending increases by approximately 30% in the two weeks before Ramadan begins, as households stock up on staples including dates, rice, lamb, yogurt, and spices." Shopping shifts to after Iftar and pre-Suhoor — MEmob's Ramadan 2026 GCC study (UAE/KSA/Qatar) finds "more than 48% of daily movement during Ramadan in the GCC happens after Iftar, compared to 18–22% on regular days," with a pronounced pre-Iftar (3–6pm) quiet-but-purposeful window for last-minute essentials. Behaviour also features bulk/stockpiling of non-perishables and strong appetite for bundles/value packs and gifting. Qatar-specific: talabat mart reported record Ramadan grocery volumes with spikes in staples. For Ayira: extend hours/ordering into the night, run Ramadan bundles (dates, juices, Vimto, water, snacks), schedule deliveries around the Iftar window, and stock up ahead of the pre-Ramadan surge.

**Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, Qatar National Day (18 Dec):** gifting, chocolate/confectionery spikes, family gatherings, celebratory/impulse buying. Plan promotions and stock around these.

**Summer seasonality.** Extreme heat (45°C+) June–September drives people indoors and toward delivery (a tailwind), but also triggers the summer expat exodus (families travel, schools out) which softens demand. Frozen/chilled logistics are hardest exactly when demand for ice cream/cold drinks peaks — cold-chain integrity is a real operational risk.

**Imagery & gender.** Keep imagery modest and product-focused; avoid culturally inappropriate model photography. Grocery purchasing is done by all genders across communities — women in many households, but also the large male migrant population shopping for themselves and sending remittances. Copy should be practical, multilingual-friendly, and family/value-oriented.

### 7. Marketing & Customer Acquisition

**Social media (near-universal; ~97% of population).** December 2025 (NapoleonCat): Facebook ~3.77m ad-reach, Messenger ~2.77m, Instagram ~1.99m (63.8% of population, 65.6% male), LinkedIn ~1.70m; TikTok reach very high (96.5% of adults per ByteDance 2023); Snapchat ~0.98m. **WhatsApp is the universal daily-communication and commerce/customer-service channel across all communities** — this is Ayira's most important channel. Platform-by-segment: Instagram/TikTok for awareness and younger/affluent; Facebook groups and community channels strong among expat communities (Indian, Filipino); WhatsApp for ordering/service/re-order; Snapchat skews Qatari/Gulf youth.

**Practical channels for a neighbourhood shop:** WhatsApp Business (catalogue, broadcast, order-taking); Google Business Profile + local SEO (people search "grocery near me / baqala + area"); Instagram for offers/new stock; expat community Facebook groups (area-specific and nationality-specific) and Qatar Living; neighbourhood flyering/QR codes on physical receipts to convert existing walk-in customers. Influencer marketing exists but is likely overkill early; micro-influencers in specific communities can be cost-effective. Loyalty: simple points/reorder incentives fit; the existing physical-shop relationship is the cheapest acquisition asset.

### 8. Shopify-Specific & UX Implications

**Grocery/convenience UX ≠ fashion UX.** Priorities: (1) fast quick-add from listing/grid without opening each product; (2) persistent, always-visible cart; (3) "Buy again"/reorder from order history (the single highest-value convenience feature — repeat baskets are near-identical); (4) strong search with brand/colloquial terms and typo tolerance; (5) shallow, well-labelled category taxonomy with clear collections; (6) list-friendly browsing and multipack variants. Dawn (Ayira's base theme) is OS 2.0 and workable but needs grocery-oriented customisation (quick-add, sticky cart, reorder app).

**Product structuring.** Use **variants** for size/pack of the same product (e.g. 330ml / 500ml / 1.5L; single / 6-pack / carton) rather than separate products — this concentrates reviews and simplifies browsing. Use **collections** for shopper missions (Beverages, Water, Soft Drinks, Juices, Ice Cream, Chocolate, Candy & Gum, Instant Noodles) and **tags** for cross-cutting filters (Halal, Gelatine-free, Sugar-free, On Offer, New). Avoid variant explosion where flavours are really distinct SKUs shoppers search for individually.

**Bilingual/RTL.** Use Shopify Markets + Translate & Adapt for English/Arabic; ensure the Dawn theme's RTL rendering is tested (menus, carousels, checkout fields, and email notifications, which need dir="rtl" added separately under Settings > Notifications). Use human Arabic copy, not machine translation, for key strings. Keep navigation structurally parallel across languages and put the language switcher in the header.

**Device & connectivity.** Qatar is mobile-first and Android-dominant (~78% Android / ~22% iOS, StatCounter 2025–26). Mobile internet is among the fastest in the world (median ~358+ Mbps, DataReportal 2025; Qatar ranks 2nd globally). Design mobile-first; ensure Apple Pay (for the affluent iOS minority) and fast mobile checkout. Internet penetration ~99%.

**App vs store.** Qataris and expats are app-heavy for delivery, but a well-built mobile web store + WhatsApp is sufficient at launch; a native app is a later, expensive step. A PWA "add to home screen" is a low-cost middle ground.

**Relevant apps:** COD app (with OTP verification to cut fake orders), delivery-date/time-slot scheduler, WhatsApp chat/order app, RTL/translation app, local courier integration (Pass/EzzyDelivery Shopify apps), and a reorder/"buy again" app.

### 9. Risks, Pitfalls & Failure Modes

- **Wrong-audience design** (building for Qataris) — the biggest risk; addressed by expat-first, English+Arabic, price-tiered approach.
- **Payments friction** — no Shopify Payments, extra Shopify fee, gateway onboarding needs the CR + e-commerce licence; COD abuse.
- **COD failed-delivery / RTO** — GCC evidence (UAE/KSA, flag as extrapolated): failed last-mile ~15% UAE / ~40% KSA; COD RTO ~20% UAE; fake COD orders a known problem. Mitigate with OTP order confirmation, mobile verification, and map-pin addresses.
- **Cold-chain in the heat** — frozen/chilled integrity at 45°C+ is a genuine failure mode; use insulated bags, short delivery radius, and time-limited frozen windows.
- **Competition response** — well-capitalised super-apps can undercut on price/speed. Meituan is subsidising aggressively: per Momentum Works, "new users of Keeta receive QAR 200 (about USD 55) in e-coupons upon registration," and Keeta's Middle East daily order volume reached ~400,000 in early 2025, rising to ~700,000 daily by November 2025 (TechBuzzChina). Ayira must not compete head-on but win on locality, service and relationship.
- **Regulatory** — operating without the new e-commerce licence is now explicitly non-compliant.
- **Market consolidation** — GCC q-commerce is consolidating (Delivery Hero absorbed InstaShop for ~US$360m; Jahez took majority control of Snoonu); standalone economics are hard, reinforcing the "own-channel + WhatsApp + existing customers" thesis over trying to become a mini-aggregator.

## Recommendations

### A. Shopify theme & UX
- **Pre-launch:** Keep Dawn but add: sticky/persistent cart, quick-add on collection pages, header search with brand-term tolerance, header language switcher (EN/AR). Build a Qatar-correct address block (Zone/Street/Building + mobile + optional map pin; postal code optional). Test RTL end-to-end.
- **First month:** Add a "Buy Again"/reorder app and order-history reorder. Add delivery time-slot scheduling.
- **First quarter:** Add PWA "add to home screen"; optimise mobile page speed; consider Arabic human-copy pass on top-selling products.

### B. Product structuring & merchandising
- **Pre-launch:** Model size/pack as variants; build mission-based collections; tag Halal/Gelatine-free/Sugar-free/Offer/New. Offer singles AND multipacks/cartons to lift AOV. Vet all confectionery for gelatine and source halal variants.
- **First month:** Introduce curated bundles (e.g. "Movie night," "Ramadan Iftar box," "Office snacks," labour-friendly "value carton" bundles) tuned to the surrounding zone's dominant segment.
- **First quarter:** Use sales data to prune dead SKUs, expand winning categories, and test auto-replenishment on high-frequency staples (water).

### C. Payments & checkout
- **Pre-launch:** Obtain the e-commerce licence (Ministerial Decision 25/2026) and CR alignment first. Integrate one primary gateway (Tap or PayTabs, or Dibsy/SADAD/QNB for Qatar-local trust) supporting cards + Apple Pay + Google Pay. Enable COD with OTP verification. Display CR/licence, returns and complaint policy per the law.
- **First month:** Monitor COD share and RTO; add mobile-number verification; set a COD cap for first-time customers.
- **First quarter:** Add Fawran/wallet options if supported; evaluate whether BNPL is worth it (likely only if AOV rises materially).

### D. Delivery operations
- **Pre-launch:** Define a tight delivery radius (dominant nearby zones). Choose model: own driver for a small radius vs a Shopify-integrated courier (Pass/EzzyDelivery) with COD collection. Set a free-delivery threshold benchmarked to QAR 99 (or lower, e.g. QAR 50–75, to beat hypermarkets locally) with a QAR 5–10 fee below it.
- **First month:** Offer same-day + scheduled windows; insulated cold-chain bags; live order status via WhatsApp.
- **First quarter:** Optimise routing/batching; review delivery-fee economics against basket margins; extend radius only if unit economics work.

### E. Marketing & acquisition
- **Pre-launch:** Set up WhatsApp Business (catalogue + ordering), Google Business Profile, Instagram. Convert existing walk-in customers with in-store QR codes/flyers and a launch offer (free first delivery, mirroring Snoonu/LuLu).
- **First month:** Post offers to relevant expat community Facebook groups and Qatar Living; run low-budget geo-targeted Instagram/Meta ads to nearby zones; start a simple loyalty/reorder incentive.
- **First quarter:** Test micro-influencers in the dominant local communities; double down on the channels with the best CAC; formalise loyalty.

### Benchmarks that would change the plan
- If **COD RTO exceeds ~15–20%**, tighten COD (prepaid incentives, stricter verification) or restrict COD zones.
- If **repeat-order rate is low (<20% within 60 days)**, the convenience/reorder proposition isn't landing — revisit assortment, delivery speed and pricing.
- If **AOV stays below the delivery break-even**, raise the free-delivery threshold or introduce bundles/minimum order.
- If **aggregator listing outperforms own-channel on contribution margin**, rebalance channel mix.
- If **cold-chain complaints spike in summer**, suspend frozen delivery in peak heat or invest in better cold-chain.

## Caveats
- **Market-size figures diverge and definitions differ** (ECDB US$330m vs Mordor US$4.54bn vs IMARC US$3.8–4.2bn for 2025 e-commerce; online grocery US$1.85bn IMARC). Growth direction is reliable; absolute numbers and forecast CAGRs (10–27%) are not directly comparable and several come from commercial research houses with limited methodology disclosure. Treat as directional.
- **COD failure/RTO rates are UAE/KSA data, explicitly extrapolated** — no Qatar-specific COD refusal rate was found. Qatar's higher banking penetration and compact geography likely make its rates lower than KSA's.
- **Segment-level monthly grocery spend for Qatar is a genuine data gap** — recommendations reason from documented income bands, not a published grocery-spend dataset.
- **Some pricing figures are indicative** (multipack, third-party retailer, or Numbeo-derived; one Indomie figure is a dated 2016 offer). Verify live QAR prices on LuLu/Carrefour/Al Meera before setting Ayira's prices.
- **Population figure (~3.17m) itself varies by source and cut-off** (World Bank ~2.86m for 2024; census 2.68m for 2020). Use the range.
- **Snoonu Prime pricing and some threshold details were not confirmed**; Talabat Pro (QAR 40/mo), Carrefour (QAR 99) and LuLu (QAR 175) thresholds are confirmed.
- Where claims rest on secondary aggregator/blog sources rather than primary data (e.g. some social-media and salary figures), they are corroborated across multiple sources but should be treated as approximate.
